<div class="defender-label">Defender</div>

<br>

The Domain Controller is protected by a **FortiEDR** collector. When the attacker's beacon executed, FortiEDR detected and blocked it automatically. Follow the steps below to investigate the incident in the **FortiEDR console**.

<div class="red">

!!! The beacon executable name is randomly generated per session. You will see a **different filename** than in the example screenshots below — look for the malicious process on the domain controller, not for the exact name shown here. !!!

</div>

<br>

1. Log in to the **FortiEDR** console (FortiCloud) <a href="Credentials" target="_blank">**credentials**</a>. Once logged in go to `Incidents` in the top navigation bar. You'll see a list of security events detected across your protected endpoints.

    Locate the incident on the **domain controller** device this is your beacon that was dropped and executed. The filename will look like a random string of characters followed by `.exe`.

    <img src="/images/EDR-incidents.jpg" alt="FortiEDR Incidents" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;">

1. Click the `+` icon on the left of the incident row to expand it. This reveals the underlying security event and its process tree, marked as **Detected**.

    Click `Investigate` on the right-hand side of the expanded event to open the full incident details.

    <img src="/images/EDR-investigate.jpg" alt="FortiEDR Investigate" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;">

1. On the incident **Overview** page, review the `Activity Audit` panel on the left. This is a full timeline of what FortiEDR did automatically, without any analyst intervention:

    - `FortiCloudServices` classified the file as **Malicious**, based on AV signature detection of your beacon file on the domain controller.
    - The **Execution Prevention** policy was violated (see the `Violated Policies` panel on the right) — flagged as *Malicious File Detected*.
    - The incident log confirms the outcome: **"beacon destroyed"** — FortiEDR terminated the malicious process before it could establish C2 communication.

    Also note the `Devices involved` and `Processes involved` panels, confirming the domain controller and your beacon process as the only assets impacted — the attack was contained to a single host.

    <img src="/images/EDR-handeld.jpg" alt="FortiEDR Activity Audit" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;">

1. Back on the **Dashboard**, note the `Simulation` toggle in the top-right corner. During this workshop the collector policies are set to **Prevention** mode (not Simulation), which is why the beacon was actively blocked and destroyed rather than just logged. If this toggle were switched to Simulation, FortiEDR would only alert on the malicious activity without blocking it.

    <img src="/images/EDR-simulation.jpg" alt="FortiEDR Simulation toggle" style="max-width: 100%; height: auto;">

<br>

**Key takeaway:** Even though the attacker successfully delivered and executed a beacon on the domain controller, FortiEDR's on-device behavioral/AV detection identified and terminated it in real time — stopping the attack chain before further lateral movement or C2 callback could occur.
